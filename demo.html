Research Connection

From phase transitions to food market crises

The Ising model describes a lattice of interacting elements — each in one of two states — that can collectively and suddenly flip from one ordered configuration to another. This phase transition is not caused by any single element: it emerges from the accumulated pressure of local interactions crossing a critical threshold.

This is the same mathematical intuition behind the CHSH and quantum decision-making work: food markets and human mobility systems exhibit analogous transitions from stability to crisis. Standard rational-actor models assume independent agents and smooth dynamics — they cannot detect the precursor signatures of these transitions. Quantum probability frameworks can, because they allow for the correlations and interference effects that characterize real decision-making under stress. The 29× difference in Bell violation ratios between crisis and non-crisis periods is precisely such a signature.

What is the Ising model?

A lattice of interacting spins (up or down) governed by nearest-neighbor coupling and temperature. Below a critical temperature, the system spontaneously orders. Above it, disorder dominates. The transition between these regimes is sharp — a phase transition.

What to explore in the demo

Adjust temperature and coupling strength and watch the system flip between ordered and disordered phases. Notice how the transition is sudden and collective — no single element causes it. That emergent criticality is the key intuition.

Connection to food systems

Food markets, trade networks, and evacuation decisions are all systems where many interacting agents can collectively and suddenly transition from stable to crisis behavior. The quantum extension (S3 framework) models the interference effects that arise when those decisions are not independent.
