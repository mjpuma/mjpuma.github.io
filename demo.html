Decisions under uncertainty

Two educational games explore context-dependent judgments and strategic choices during hypothetical food-supply disruptions. Classical models provide a baseline; quantum probability offers a possible mathematical framework for certain context effects. These are illustrations, not empirical findings or crisis forecasts.

Question order · Context · Probability

The Order Matters

You are advising a government during a grain shortage. Answer two questions, then reverse their order. Explore a hypothetical mathematical prediction below. This is not a randomized survey.

Illustrative quantum-probability model

Vary the angle between two question projectors. The initial state is defined as “Yes” to question A. These are hypothetical mathematical probabilities, not observations or predictions of your responses.

Explore the mathematics

Quantum-probability models can represent binary questions by projectors PA and PB. The sequential probability ||P_B P_A |ψ⟩||² can differ from ||P_A P_B |ψ⟩||² when the projectors do not commute. This is a mathematical modeling possibility, not evidence of physical quantum cognition.

Classical context-sensitive models can also produce order effects. Discriminating models requires data and specific testable predictions, such as the QQ equality.

Wang et al. (2014), PNAS

Strategic decisions · Game theory

The Trade Dilemma

Two hypothetical grain-exporting countries face a shortage. Each can maintain exports or restrict them. Choose Country A's action and adjust your belief about Country B's behavior.

Explore the mathematics

Classical baseline: If p is the probability that B restricts, then Country A's expected payoffs are U(M) = 3(1−p) and U(R) = 4(1−p) + p. At zero extra cost, restriction is strictly dominant in the baseline matrix and (Restrict, Restrict) is its Nash equilibrium. With an extra cost, the displayed matrix is no longer the full adjusted game; the calculator varies A’s incentives only. These numbers are not calibrated to real countries.

Quantum-probability opportunity: One could investigate whether contextual representations of beliefs and sequential judgments yield testable improvements over classical behavioral models. This game does not compute quantum equilibria, entanglement, or quantum advantage.

Yearsley & Busemeyer (2016), quantum cognition tutorial

Scope: Illustrative educational games only. The games do not intentionally transmit or store responses; ordinary website hosting and third-party services may process connection data. Neither game implies physical quantum effects in brains or validated predictions of food-market crises.
